---
title: Investment accounts
description: Invested value and available cash, repriced by the market through dated price observations.
---

An investment account holds value put to work for a return: shares, funds,
bonds, anything a market reprices over time. The type exists because invested
value carries two numbers that must stay distinct: the balance, which is what
the ledger says you moved in and out, and the estimation, which is what the
market says the position is worth.

## Positions

Each investment in the account is a position with an initial quantity, the
same opening role a starting balance plays for an account. Units then move
only through transaction legs: a purchase into the position carries the
quantity bought, a sale out of it carries the quantity sold. The quantity
held at a date is the initial quantity plus every landed purchase minus every
landed sale. Activities scheduled in the future are previsions; their units
do not count until their date arrives.

## The price timeline

A price is a dated observation of the unit price, not a field that gets
overwritten. The price in effect at a date is the latest observation at or
before it, so history is kept and a position can be valued at any past date.
Between observations, the last price stands.

## Estimation

The account summary shows the market estimation under the balance: the sum
over priced positions of quantity held times latest price. It carries the
date of the observation it rests on and the unrealized difference against the
balance, and when only some positions are priced it says how many were
counted. When nothing is priced there is no estimation at all; absence is
never shown as zero.

An estimation is a measurement, not a ledger event. It never produces a
transaction and never becomes revenue. The difference is realized when you
sell, as a normal activity.

## Cash in the account

Uninvested money belongs to the account too. Investment accounts carry a
starting cash balance, kept distinct from the starting balance so available
cash stays visible next to invested value.

## Investment or asset?

The test is why you hold the thing. Held for return, divisible into units,
and repriced by a market: an investment, even at exactly one unit, as with a
rental property whose appraisals become price points. Held for use: an
[Asset](/docs/financial/account-types/assets).
