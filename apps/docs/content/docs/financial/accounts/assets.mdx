---
title: Assets
description: Objects owned and used, carried at cost, with optional dated valuations.
---

An asset is a singular object you own and use: a car, a home, equipment. The
type exists for value that is neither cash nor a market position, and whose
book value comes from what you put into it rather than from what a market
says.

## Book value

An asset's value in the ledger is the money it received minus the money it
gave, through transaction legs tagged with the asset. Buying the car moves
value into it; depreciation and a sale are the only scheduled changes its book
value ever receives.

## Depreciation

An object you use wears out, and depreciation spreads that consumption over
time as plain ledger activities. A plan states the amount to spread, the
number of monthly installments, the first month, and the expense account the
installments are booked to. Editing a plan regenerates the installments it
still owns, from today forward; past installments stay frozen history.

Depreciation is arithmetic, not prediction: a known amount spread over a
known period, booking your use of the object.

## Estimated value

Observations of worth are a different kind of fact from bookkeeping. An
appraisal is dated, external, and evidence-backed, so valuations form a
timeline like investment prices: the latest valuation at or before a date is
the estimated value then, shown as the Estimation next to the balance in the
account summary. A valuation is never booked. An object that gained value
produced no income; the gain is visible as a number, and only a sale realizes
it.

There is deliberately no reverse depreciation. A schedule that grows a value
by assumption would book income nobody paid, and would keep claiming it in
the months the market disagrees.

## Asset or investment?

See [Investment accounts](/docs/financial/accounts/investment-accounts).
The same house can be either: lived in, it is an asset carried at cost;
genuinely held for return, it is a position whose appraisals are price points.
