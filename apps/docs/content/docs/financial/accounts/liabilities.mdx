---
title: Liabilities
description: Amounts owed, carried as balances until repaid.
---

A liability account holds what you owe: a loan, a credit line, an unpaid
balance. The type exists so debt is a first-class part of the ledger instead
of a note in an account name. A loan taken moves value from the liability
account to the account that received the money, and each repayment moves
value back. The balance, derived from the same transactions as every other
balance, is what remains owed.

## Sharing

A shared liability is not inferred from the account or its balance. Who owes
what is calculated from the shared activity transactions and mappings
described in
[Categories, projects & sharing](/docs/financial/categories-projects-and-sharing),
so the split changes only when the underlying transactions change.

## Implications of each action

- **Record a loan received** — value moves from the liability account to the
  account that received the money. Your net worth is unchanged: the bank
  balance and the debt grew by the same amount, and the ledger shows both.
- **Record a repayment** — value moves back, reducing what is owed. Split the
  repayment across transactions when interest and principal must land in
  different places, as one activity.
- **Edit the starting balance** — corrects the opening amount owed at the
  ledger boundary; every derived figure of the account shifts with it.
- **Delete the account** — removes the debt record **and** its transaction
  history, including the repayments that proved it was shrinking. See
  [Accounts](/docs/financial/accounts).
