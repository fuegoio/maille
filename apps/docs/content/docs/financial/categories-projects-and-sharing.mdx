---
title: Categories, projects & sharing
description: Add reporting and shared-liability dimensions without changing the ledger.
---

Transactions determine balances. Categories, projects, contacts, and
counterparties add context around those transactions. Keeping context separate
means you can reorganize reporting without rewriting the financial event.

## Categories and subcategories

A category and optional subcategory classify an activity. They answer questions
such as “How much did I spend on housing?” without becoming accounts themselves.

This distinction matters:

- the Expense account establishes that value was consumed;
- the category explains the kind of expense;
- changing the category does not change either account balance.

Categories form a two-level reporting hierarchy. Use broad categories for stable
reporting and subcategories for detail you expect to compare.

## Projects

A project groups activities that belong to the same real-world effort or period,
even when their categories differ. A move, a renovation, or a trip can contain
transport, fees, equipment, and meals while still being reviewed as one project.

Projects can define start and end dates. Those dates describe the project's
scope; the activity date still determines when the transaction affects the
ledger.

## Contacts and counterparties

A **contact** is another Maille user connected to you. A **counterparty** is the
ledger-side identity used in transactions and can be mapped to a contact.
Separating them allows a financial relationship to remain explicit even when it
is not tied to another Maille account.

## Shared liability

Account-sharing records identify primary and secondary relationships and retain
a proportion. The current shared-liability calculation is derived from shared
activity transactions, counterparties, and mapped accounts — not from a manually
entered summary balance.

Transaction direction matters: value flowing from or to a counterparty changes
that contact's computed liability. The activity then exposes both the liability
and the affected account amounts.

## Why these are not accounts

Turning every reporting label or person into a balance account would overload
the ledger with concepts that answer different questions. Maille instead keeps:

- accounts for location and financial role;
- categories for reporting;
- projects for cross-cutting scope;
- counterparties and contacts for ownership and liability;
- funds for purpose.

That separation is what lets one activity participate in several useful views
without duplicating its transactions.
