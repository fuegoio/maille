---
title: Funds
description: Track what money is for without confusing purpose with location.
---

Accounts answer **where money is**. Funds answer **what money is for**. Maille
keeps those dimensions independent because a purpose can span several accounts,
and one account can hold money reserved for several purposes.

A Vacation fund, for example, may include part of a savings account and part of
a current account. Moving the cash between those accounts should not erase its
purpose.

## Fund balances

A fund balance is calculated as:

```text
opening allocations + inflows - outflows
```

It is not a second account balance. The same underlying money remains in its
bank, cash, or investment account while the fund records its intended use.

## Opening allocations

A fund can allocate opening amounts from several accounts. The allocation takes
effect on the fund's start date, falling back to — and never preceding — the
ledger starting date.

Opening allocations describe purpose at the historical boundary. They do not
move money between accounts.

## Fund legs on transactions

Transactions can carry fund legs with an amount, date, optional note, and two
sides:

- `fromFund` — the purpose losing the amount;
- `toFund` — the purpose receiving the amount.

Either side can be null. A null side represents **Untracked** or a crossing of
the tracked balance-sheet boundary:

| From      | To        | Meaning                                  |
| --------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Untracked | Vacation  | Assign existing money to Vacation        |
| Vacation  | Untracked | Release or consume Vacation money        |
| Vacation  | Emergency | Reassign purpose without moving accounts |

## Hierarchy

Funds can form a tree. A parent fund's tree balance includes all descendants.
Moves between funds inside the same subtree cancel when the subtree is viewed as
a whole; only amounts crossing its boundary change the tree total.

Cycles are rejected. A fund cannot become its own parent or be placed below one
of its descendants.

## Why over-allocation is rejected

Assigning more money to funds than the underlying account position supports
would make the purpose view contradict the balance sheet. Maille rejects that
state. Money without a fund remains visible as **Untracked** rather than being
silently assigned.

<Callout>
  A transfer between bank accounts and a transfer between funds answer different questions. Record
  the account transaction for location and the fund leg for purpose when both change.
</Callout>
