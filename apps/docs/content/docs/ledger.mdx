---
title: The ledger model
description: Accounts, activities, transactions, movements, and funds — one model, zero approximation.
---

Everything in Maille derives from one strict double-entry ledger. This page
defines each part of the model and how the parts relate. If you understand this
page, you can trace any number on screen back to the transactions underneath.

## Accounts

An account is a place money can live or flow through. Maille distinguishes:

- **Balance accounts** — `bank_account`, `investment_account`, `cash`, `assets`,
  and `liabilities`.
- **Profit-and-loss accounts** — `expense` and `revenue`.

Accounts carry a `startingBalance` (and a separate `startingCashBalance`), can be
flagged to accept movements, and can be **shared** with a contact. Sharing
records contain a `primary`/`secondary` role and a proportion; shared liability
is computed from shared activity transactions, counterparties, and mapped
accounts.

## Activities and transactions

An **activity** is a real-world event: a salary, a grocery run, a rent payment.
It has a status — `scheduled`, `incomplete`, or `completed` — and may contain
zero or more transactions. Each transaction moves an amount from one account to
another. This is the double-entry core of Maille:

- a revenue lands as transactions between a revenue account and a bank account;
- an expense draws from a bank account into an expense account;
- a transfer moves money between two personal accounts without touching revenue
  or expense.

Each transaction contributes equal and opposite account movements. Account
balances also include independently entered starting balances.

Activities are organized by **categories** and **subcategories**, can be tied to
a **project**, and can involve **contacts** for shared expenses.

## Movements and reconciliation

A **movement** is a line from the outside world: a bank statement entry. It has
a status — `incomplete` or `completed` — and starts its life unlinked.

**Reconciliation is explicit**: links record which activities explain a
movement and how much each activity contributes. A movement is `completed` when
the sum of its linked amounts equals the movement amount; otherwise it remains
`incomplete`.

Reconciliation is many-to-many. An activity can explain several movements, and
a movement can be split across several activities. Each link records the amount
attributed to that activity; the account totals determine whether both sides are
fully reconciled.

## Funds

A **fund** labels money by _purpose_, independently of the _account_ where it
lives. "Vacation savings" is a fund; the money might sit across a savings
account and a cash envelope.

- Funds form a **tree**: a fund has a `parentFund` (null for a root), and parent
  chains never form cycles.
- Transactions may carry **fund legs** with an amount and one or both of
  `fromFund` and `toFund`; a null side represents Untracked or the balance-sheet
  boundary.
- A fund may have **opening allocations** across multiple accounts. They take
  effect on the fund's start date, falling back to — and never preceding — the
  user's starting date.

Fund positions are replayed from starting balances, opening allocations,
transactions, and fund legs. Over-allocation is rejected; unallocated money
remains Untracked.

## Assets and depreciation

An asset (a car, a home) is a record linked to an `assets` account. Its value is
the net total of asset-tagged transaction flows. A `linear` depreciation
schedule generates monthly ledger activities.

## What this buys you

| Question                             | Where the answer comes from                               |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is my balance?                  | Transactions on personal accounts, plus starting balances |
| Did the bank agree?                  | Movements reconciled to activities                        |
| What is this money for?              | Fund allocations computed from fund moves                 |
| What do I owe on the shared account? | Shared transactions, counterparties, and mapped accounts  |
| What is my car worth today?          | Net asset-tagged transaction flows                        |
