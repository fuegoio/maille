---
title: Correctness
description: Derivation, equality checks, and the invalid states Maille refuses to enter.
---

A ledger is only as trustworthy as its guarantees. Maille's correctness model
rests on four ideas: derive instead of store, check equality exactly, keep an
append-only record, and refuse states that would make two views disagree.

## Derived, not stored

Every total you see is computed from dated records at read time:

- account balances from starting balances plus transactions;
- activity types and per-type amounts from the account types of transaction
  legs;
- fund balances and per-account fund positions from a replay of allocations
  and fund legs;
- shared liability from counterparty legs and mapped accounts;
- investment quantities from initial quantities plus leg quantities.

Nothing that can be derived is stored, so no two stored numbers can disagree.
The cost is computation; the benefit is that a display bug cannot corrupt the
ledger, and an edit anywhere updates everything that depends on it, instantly
and consistently.

## Equality, not approximation

Where two things should be equal, Maille compares them as an equality, within
a two-decimal rounding:

- a movement is complete when its linked amounts sum to its statement amount;
- an activity is complete when, for every movement-tracked account it touches,
  the transaction total equals the linked movement total;
- fund legs may not exceed the amount of the transaction they travel with;
- a fund allocation may not exceed what the account's position supports.

These are checks, not confidence scores. There is no fuzzy matching that
quietly declares a 90% match good enough — a record is complete only when
the amounts account for each other exactly, and everything else stays visibly
incomplete.

## Invariants the model cannot violate

Some states would make two views contradict each other, and the mutation
layer rejects them outright:

- **Over-allocated funds.** Assigning more money to funds than the underlying
  accounts hold would make the purpose view disagree with the balance sheet.
  Money without a fund stays visibly **Untracked** instead of being silently
  absorbed.
- **Cycles in the fund tree.** A fund cannot become its own parent or be
  moved under one of its descendants; a cycle has no root and no readable
  tree balance.
- **A fund start date after transactions already allocated to it.** Moving
  the boundary behind existing flows would orphan them.
- **Fund legs beyond their transaction.** A purpose leg is an annotation on
  money that provably moved, so it cannot describe more money than the
  transaction carried.

## Append-only history

Activities and movements keep a change log: an append-only timeline of what
was edited, from what, to what. Corrections in a ledger should not look like
nothing happened — the current state stays clean, and the record of how it got
there stays inspectable. This is the personal-finance version of an audit
trail, and it is why you can edit freely without losing the ability to answer
"when did this change?".

## What this implies for how you work

- **Edit fearlessly.** Changing a transaction recomputes every dependent
  number. There is no "recalculate" step to forget.
- **Trust incompleteness.** An incomplete record is not an error — it is the
  system telling you a proof is still owed. Work the queues until they are
  empty, and then every completed record is backed by evidence.
- **Expect refusals.** A rejected allocation or fund move is the model
  protecting a guarantee. The fix is to record the underlying fact, not to
  force the label.
- **Audit anything.** Any balance, report total, or fund position can be
  traced down to the dated entries that produced it — that traceability is
  the product.
