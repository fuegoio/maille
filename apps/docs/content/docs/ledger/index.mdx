A ledger is a record of changes to positions, kept so that every total can be
re-derived from the changes that produced it. Bookkeeping has used this shape
for centuries: a list of dated entries, each entry naming the value it moves,
and balances computed by replaying the entries. Maille applies that discipline
to personal finance, where most tools instead store summaries — a balance
field, a spent-this-month counter — that slowly stop agreeing with the
transactions behind them.

This section explains the concepts before the features. Read it once and the
rest of Maille becomes predictable: every screen is a different projection of
the same records.

## The ledger and the two other kinds of facts

Maille keeps three kinds of facts, and never converts one into another:

| Kind        | Records                                            | Answers                   | Booked into balances? |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Ledger      | Activities, transactions, fund legs                | What happened, and why    | Yes                   |
| Evidence    | Movements (bank and broker statement lines), links | What the institution said | No, only compared     |
| Observation | Investment prices, asset valuations                | What the thing is worth   | No, only displayed    |

A statement line is evidence about money; it is not an explanation of it. A
market price is a measurement of a position; it is not income. The ledger
records what you concluded happened, and the other two kinds of facts either
corroborate it or describe it. Confusing the three is how approximations enter
a finance tool, and refusing to confuse them is the core of Maille's design.

## Why a personal ledger and not a spending app

A spending app answers one question well — what did I spend this month — by
classifying statement lines. A ledger answers a family of questions from one
set of records:

- **Where is my money and how much do I owe?** Balance accounts and liability
  accounts, valued by replaying transactions.
- **What changed it?** Flow accounts: every change entered through a revenue or
  expense account, or moved between balance accounts as a transfer.
- **Does my record agree with the bank?** Reconciliation: the ledger total per
  account, compared against the movements the institution reported.
- **What is the money for?** Funds: a dimension over the money, not a
  duplication of it.
- **What did it used to be?** Because balances are derived from dated entries,
  every past date can be recomputed exactly.

None of this requires you to be an accountant. It requires only that every
event you record says where value came from and where it went — the idea the
next page makes precise.
