---
title: Time & previsions
description: When an entry affects balances, where history begins, and what future-dated entries mean.
---

A ledger is a time series, not a spreadsheet. Every transaction belongs to a
date, and every balance is a position **at** a date. Maille derives any
balance by replaying the transactions dated at or before the moment you ask
about, which is what makes "what did I have in March?" an exact question, not
a reconstruction.

## The activity date is the ledger date

A transaction takes its date from its activity. That single date decides:

- when the transaction starts affecting account balances;
- whether it is included in a period report;
- whether it counts in a position or quantity today.

Other dates in Maille are context, not ledger time. A project's start and end
dates describe the scope of an effort; they do not move the transactions that
belong to it. A movement's date is the institution's claim about when value
moved, compared against the ledger but never substituted for it. When you
reconcile, the amounts must agree; the dates may legitimately differ by a day
or two.

## The starting date is the boundary of history

The ledger's starting date answers "history begins here":

- transactions dated before it are excluded from balances and reports;
- account starting balances describe each account at that boundary;
- a fund without its own start date uses the ledger starting date for its
  opening allocations.

The starting date is what keeps imported history honest. Everything before
the boundary is summarized by opening balances; everything after it is
explained by transactions. There is no synthetic "opening transaction", and
therefore no fake event in your activity list pretending something happened.

Moving the starting date later can exclude transactions that previously
counted; moving it earlier exposes a period your opening balances may not
describe. Treat it as a decision about where truth begins, and set it once,
early.

## Future entries are previsions

An activity whose date is in the future is **scheduled**: its transactions
exist, but they do not affect any balance, quantity, or fund position until
their date arrives. This is a prevision — a statement of intent in the same
shape as a fact, so that when the day comes it becomes true without being
re-entered.

| Date of activity            | Status     | Counted in balances |
| --------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------- |
| Future                      | Scheduled  | No                  |
| Today or past, reconciled   | Completed  | Yes                 |
| Today or past, unreconciled | Incomplete | Yes                 |

Note the last row: an incomplete activity still affects balances. Its
transactions are recorded facts; what is incomplete is the proof against the
bank, not the ledger entry itself. The status dimension says "you have not
verified this against a statement yet", never "this did not happen".

## Monthly conventions

Some ledger events are generated rather than entered. Depreciation
installments land on the first of each month, and investment prices and asset
valuations are dated observations on a timeline. Both follow the same reading
rule as balances: the value in effect at a date is the latest observation at
or before it, and observations after the date you ask about are invisible — a
future price is a prevision, not a valuation.

## Implications of dating choices

- **Dating an activity on the wrong day** misplaces it in every period report
  and every historical balance. The date is load-bearing; prefer the day value
  actually moved.
- **Backdating an activity** instantly changes all balances and reports
  computed after that date, including already-read ones. Nothing else needs to
  be edited, which is the point of derivation.
- **Scheduling an activity far in the future** keeps it visible and counted
  nowhere until it arrives. Editing its amount while scheduled is free; the
  ledger only feels it when the date passes.
- **Editing a fund's start date** re-dates its opening allocations, and is
  rejected if it would move the fund's boundary after transactions already
  allocated to it.
